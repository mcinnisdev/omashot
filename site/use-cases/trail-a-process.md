# A process, trailed

The thing you do every month, that only you know how to do. Writing it down
means doing it while narrating every click, and you will skip the step you
stopped noticing years ago.

A **trail** captures while you work, so the record is of what you actually did
rather than what you remembered doing.

## Do it

1. <kbd>Super</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>T</kbd>, then do the thing at normal
   speed.
2. <kbd>Super</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>T</kbd> again to stop. The brief opens
   on everything it caught.
3. Cut the noise — the shots of you finding the window, the duplicate of the
   same screen. Note the ones that survive. Drag any that landed out of order.
4. <kbd>Super</kbd><kbd>Alt</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> to split it into sections, one
   per stage.

Every frame is an ordinary shot: markup, notes, reordering, deletion. There is
nothing special about them because they came from a trail.

## Then either

**Hand it to an agent** with the purpose set to *Write process doc*, and it
turns your notes into prose with the images in place.

**Or skip the agent**: **Hand off → Export document** writes the finished
document straight from the brief — sections become headings, notes become
numbered steps, images sit under each. See
[a document that writes itself](/use-cases/write-the-doc).

## No video

A trail makes stills, not a recording, because an agent cannot read a video
and a person reading a process document does not want to scrub one. If a
person needs to watch it happen, that is
[a walkthrough](/use-cases/a-walkthrough).

::: warning On Wayland, a trail runs on a timer
Firing a shot on every click needs `/dev/input` access Wayland does not hand
out, so a trail currently captures at an interval. Work slowly and check what
it caught before you stop.
:::
